\paragraph{A2 -- How much contamination is ``a lot''?} From kinetic theory, a molecule of mass
\SI{150}{u} present at \SI{1}{ppb} strikes the surface at \SI{1.3e18}{m^{-2}.s^{-1}}. That is a
monolayer-equivalent every \SI{\sim8}{s}. If only one impact in a million is photo-fixed, a monolayer
still grows every \SI{\sim90}{days} at \SI{1}{ppb}, every \SI{\sim9}{days} at \SI{10}{ppb} and about
daily at \SI{100}{ppb}. Parts-per-billion levels of organics are therefore enough to explain degradation
over days to weeks. This is consistent with the Aeolus experience~\cite{ESA2018Aeolus}, and it is why the
purge gas needs a purifier and the cells must be clean.

\paragraph{A3 -- How thick must a film be to matter?} An absorbing film with extinction coefficient $k$
absorbs \SI{10}{\percent} at a thickness of \SI{103}{nm}, \SI{31}{nm} and \SI{10}{nm} for $k=0.03$, $0.1$ and
$0.3$ (polymer-like to graphitic carbon; the values of $k$ are assumptions). A transparent film
($n=1.5$) whose thickness follows the Gaussian intensity profile reduces the coupling efficiency as
$\eta=\mathrm{sinc}^2(\phi_0/2)$, where $\phi_0$ is the peak phase. A \SI{10}{\percent} loss needs
$\phi_0=\SI{1.1}{rad}$, i.e.\ a peak thickness of \SI{\approx130}{nm}. Absorbing films of a few tens of
nanometres, or transparent ones of about a hundred, are therefore enough. Both are easy to see by AFM or
white-light interferometry.

\paragraph{A4 -- Heating by an absorbing film} For absorption at the surface of a semi-infinite silica
body, the steady-state temperature rise at the centre is $\Delta T=P_\text{abs}/(\sqrt{2\pi}\,\kappa w)$
with $\kappa=\SI{1.38}{W/(m.K)}$~\cite{Lax1977}. A film that absorbs \SI{10}{\percent} of \SI{3}{mW}
heats the face by \SI{\approx70}{K}, and at \SI{10}{mW} by \SI{\approx240}{K}. Once a film absorbs, it
heats itself and its surroundings. This feedback can accelerate its own growth and explains why
degradation often appears to ``run away''.

\paragraph{A6 -- Continuous purge} A well-mixed cell of volume $V$ purged with $Q$ approaches the feed
composition with time constant $V/Q$. For $V=\SI{30}{cm^3}$ and $Q=\SI{20}{sccm}$ (time constant
\SI{1.5}{min}) the ideal time to reach \SI{10}{ppm} is \SI{15}{min}. Allowing a factor of three for
imperfect mixing, start measurements no earlier than an hour after closing the cell. The steady-state
\Oxy{} fraction is set by the influx divided by $Q$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item permeation through dense printed walls (\SI{60}{cm^2}, \SI{3}{mm}; PET \SI{0.034}{Barrer} to
        PP \SI{1.7}{Barrer}, \cref{tab:materials}) adds only \SIrange{0.1}{6.5}{ppm} at \SI{5}{sccm};
  \item one metre of \numproduct{6 x 4}\,mm polyurethane, PTFE or FEP tubing adds
        \SIrange{70}{130}{ppm} at \SI{5}{sccm}, nylon \SI{\sim4}{ppm}, and stainless steel nothing.
        \emph{Tubing, not the printed cell, limits the purity}.
  \item Water is a different matter. At \SI{50}{\percent} lab RH, \SI{0.2}{mg/day} of water passes the same
        wall in PET or PEEK, but \SI{3.8}{mg/day} in PLA or ABS (about \SI{650}{ppmv} at \SI{5}{sccm}).
  \item Back-diffusion through a \SI{30}{cm} long, \SI{2}{mm} bore outlet is suppressed by $\exp(-\mathrm{Pe})$,
        with Péclet number $\mathrm{Pe}=QL/(AD)\approx40$ even at \SI{0.5}{sccm} ($D_{\ce{O2}\text{--}\ce{N2}}=\SI{0.20}{cm^2/s}$~\cite{Marrero1972}). A modest outlet line
        is therefore as effective as a bubbler, provided the flow never stops.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{A7 -- Static absorber cell} A sealed \SI{30}{cm^3} cell of air contains \SI{6.3}{cm^3} of
\Oxy. Absorbing it lowers the pressure of a rigid cell by \SI{\approx210}{mbar}, which then sucks air in
through any leak. After an \Ntwo{} pre-purge to \SI{1}{\percent} \Oxy{} only \SI{0.3}{cm^3} remains and the
pressure drop is \SI{\approx10}{mbar}. The absorber then has to cope mainly with leaks and permeation.
A ZP-50-size sachet (\SI{50}{cm^3} of \Oxy) lasts about 8 years at an air leak rate of
\SI{1e-6}{mbar.L/s}, about 9 months at \SI{1e-5}{mbar.L/s} and only about 4 weeks at
\SI{1e-4}{mbar.L/s}. Hence the leak-rate specification of \SI{1e-5}{mbar.L/s} in \cref{sec:cells}.
Permeation adds to the load: a \SI{2}{mm} FKM or butyl O-ring of \SI{30}{mm} diameter lets in about
\SI{2}{\micro L} of \Oxy{} per day (\SI{+65}{ppm/day} in a sealed \SI{30}{cm^3} cell without absorber), a
silicone O-ring about 200 times more. With an absorber, the equilibrium concentration in a leaking box is
$[\Oxy]\approx L/(12.7\,C)$~\cite{Lambert1992}. An ingress of \SI{200}{ppm/day} and $C=50/30$ give
about \SI{10}{ppm}, comfortably below the \SI{100}{ppm} target, as long as the absorber is not exhausted.
Water ingress, by contrast, saturates an undesiccated cell within hours (A6). A pressure-decay test with a
\SI{0.1}{mbar} sensor over \SI{12}{h} resolves \SI{\sim1e-7}{mbar.L/s}. A temperature change of only
\SI{0.1}{K} shifts the pressure by \SI{0.34}{mbar}, so temperature must be logged and corrected for.

\paragraph{A8 -- Optics} Mode matching to a \SI{2.4}{\micro m} mode needs collimated beam diameters of
\SI{0.88}{mm}, \SI{0.98}{mm} and \SI{1.57}{mm} for $f=\SI{4.5}{mm}$, \SI{5}{mm} and \SI{8}{mm}
($w_\text{in}=\lambda f/\pi w_0$~\cite{Kogelnik1966}). A \SI{1}{mm} fused-silica window
($n=1.474$~\cite{Malitson1965}) in a beam converging at NA~0.1 adds \SI{5}{nm} of primary spherical
aberration (Strehl ratio $>0.9999$) and moves the focus by \SI{0.32}{mm}. A bare end face reflects
\SI{3.7}{\percent}. At an \ang{8} APC face the input beam must arrive \ang{3.8} off the fibre axis, and
the reflection leaves \ang{23.7} away from the incoming beam. A coreless end cap of length $L$ expands
the beam before the glass--gas interface (Rayleigh range in glass \SI{18}{\micro m}). This reduces the
face intensity 32-fold for $L=\SI{100}{\micro m}$, 124-fold for \SI{200}{\micro m}, 277-fold for
\SI{300}{\micro m} and 770-fold for \SI{500}{\micro m}.

\paragraph{A9 -- How small a degradation rate can be demonstrated?} For $N$ daily readings over $T$ days
with scatter $\sigma$, the standard error of a fitted slope is $\sigma/(\sqrt{N}\,T/\sqrt{12})$. With
$\sigma=\SI{0.3}{\percent}$, three weeks give a 95\,\% upper limit of \SI{\approx0.9}{\percent} per
\SI{1000}{h}, and six weeks give \SI{\approx0.3}{\percent} per \SI{1000}{h}. The ``degradation
halted'' criterion of \cref{sec:analysis} ($<\SI{1}{\percent}$ per \SI{1000}{h}) is therefore within
reach of a single long run, provided systematic drifts are controlled to the same level. Controlling
them is the real challenge.
